Scientific Objects

Two candidates, different questions

Research candidates and proposed tests are not validated scientific findings. Original records retain their historical wording.

Candidate A · Registry discoverability

Proposed hypothesis

Can isolated identification and verification registries be transformed into contextually discoverable information while preserving authority, provenance, governance and semantic meaning?

Historical and generalized scope

Source-supported distinction

The earliest recorded research question specifically concerned travellers. A broader actor–seeker scope is a subsequent proposed extension, not a change to that historical question.

Candidate B · Intersection formation

Proposed hypothesis

Can independently evidenced actor information pathways and evolving seeker information needs form a meaningful intersection beyond what existing information retrieval and graph methods already explain?

Operational distinctions

Definition requirement

Information existence, relevance, discoverability, encounter, recognition, economic feasibility and actual market access are different observations. An actor mention does not demonstrate an economic opportunity relationship.

Relationship between objects

Conceptual dependency

Registry-derived information may be an input to intersection experiments, but registry discoverability and intersection formation have different units of analysis, hypotheses and falsifiers.

What would challenge either object

Competing explanations

Conventional indexing, federation, metadata improvement, entity resolution, knowledge graphs and existing interoperability protocols may account for the same results.
