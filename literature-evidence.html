Literature & Evidence

Prior art, competing explanations and open questions

Research candidates and proposed tests are not validated scientific findings. Original records retain their historical wording.

Information seeking and affordances

Established research lenses

Information seeking, exploratory search, relevance and affordance theories offer explanations for how people encounter and interpret information. Their applicability to a new mechanism must be tested rather than presumed.

FAIR and interoperability

Established research lenses

Findability, accessibility, interoperability, reusability, metadata and provenance offer relevant design principles; implementing these principles alone would not establish doctoral novelty.

Findability and federation

Prior-art comparison

Published work on findability metrics and federated search provides direct alternatives to claims that cross-source information discovery is unexplored.

Entity resolution and query answering

Prior-art comparison

Research on collective entity resolution, record linkage errors and query answering addresses cross-source identity and semantic consistency; any proposed contribution must be compared against these methods.

Trust and governance

Prior-art comparison

Trust-scheme interoperability and verification semantics constrain how registry evidence can be reused without silently changing its meaning or authority.

Evidence quality and gaps

Audit limitation

The citation registry contains catalogued literature with mixed verification levels. Abstract-level checks, full-text analysis, independent replication and peer-review status must not be treated as equivalent.
